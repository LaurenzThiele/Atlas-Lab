About this map

Tsunami records that NCEI rates definite or probable (validity 3 or more), with coordinates and a wave height above 0 m.

The timeline is scaled by the number of tsunamis, not by years: each of the 1,124 tsunamis gets the same stretch of the axis, and all its records appear together. Most tsunamis on record are recent, so 684 to 1900 takes up the first sixth of the axis and the years since 2000 the last third. A large tsunami arrives as one burst: the 2011 Tohoku tsunami left 6,193 records, a fifth of all of them. The year labels sit where their year begins.

Download the data

The layers exactly as the map loads them, in JSON.

  • Tsunami records: all 29,340 records as columns of place, coordinates, date, wave height, deaths and NCEI tsunami number. NOAA NCEI, public domain.
  • First, tallest, deadliest: the three red records, plus every record of the 2004 tsunami. NOAA NCEI, public domain.
  • Regions: the NCEI region codes and their names. NOAA NCEI, public domain.
  • Pacific rim order: the bearing of every Pacific record seen from the middle of the ocean. NOAA NCEI, public domain.
  • Figures: the numbers in this sheet, each with how it was computed. NOAA NCEI, public domain.
  • Land: Natural Earth 1:50m land as plain polygons. Natural Earth, public domain.

The original downloads come from their sources: NOAA NCEI Global Historical Tsunami Database and Natural Earth 1:50m land. Rebuild these layers from them with uv run maps/prepare.py (see the README).
