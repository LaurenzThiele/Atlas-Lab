About this map

The 2D map shows how many people live in each place in the GHS-POP 2025 estimate. It is smoothed over about 8 km, so the inhabited strip looks wider than it really is: set the slider to 95% to see only the squares that hold 95% of people, and zoom in past level 9 to see the real 1 km squares. To find those squares, I start with the most crowded square kilometre, add the next most crowded, and keep going until the squares hold 95% of Egypt's people. That takes 22,090 km² of Egypt's 1,005,130 km², or 2.2%. Counted in smaller 100 m squares, the same people fit on 0.9% of the land, because the fields, roads and river between homes no longer count. The colours of the squares and columns step at 1,000, 5,000 and 20,000 people per km². In 3D each 1 km square rises by its people per km² (1 km of height for every 4,000 people per km²); squares under 100 people per km² stay flat. Rivers and lakes outside Egypt are drawn lighter. The Nile's 1,230 km is its main stem inside Egypt, without the delta branches. The border is Natural Earth's, which includes the Halaib triangle.

The layers this map draws

  • egypt_population_1km.csv: every 1 km square with people (lon, lat, people, per_km2, cum_share, in_95). GHS-POP, CC BY 4.0. Contains modified GHS-POP R2023A data, © European Union, 1995-2026, licensed under CC BY 4.0.
  • land.geojson: land and sea. Natural Earth, public domain.
  • egypt_border.geojson: the Egypt border. Natural Earth, public domain.
  • nile_lakes.geojson: the Nile, rivers and lakes. Natural Earth, public domain.

The other layers (the 1 km squares as binary arrays, the share curve behind the slider and the dots, which are a random drawing, not data) are built from GHS-POP R2023A with uv run maps/prepare.py.
