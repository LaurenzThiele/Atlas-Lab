About this scene

A model of Switzerland cut along one straight line through Basel and the Dufourspitze, 185 km from the Rhine on the German border to Monte Rosa on the Italian border. The heights come from the Copernicus 30 m elevation model, one every 30 m along the line, and are drawn 9 times taller in 3D and 17 times taller in the side view. A 30 m grid reads sharp summits a little low, so the height of the Dufourspitze is the official one from the swisstopo map.

Sources

Gives the corridor relief, the profile and every height on the line. Copernicus DEM licence, free for any use with the notice below. https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/ (licence: https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM)
  2. ESA and the Copernicus programme, served by the AWS Open Data Registry (Sinergise): Copernicus DEM GLO-90, 2021 release, about 90 m. Gives the relief of the base map. Same licence and link.
  3. Federal Office of Topography swisstopo: swissBOUNDARIES3D, release 2026-01. Gives the Swiss border, where the line starts and ends. Free geodata, open use with the source named. https://data.geo.admin.ch/ch.swisstopo.swissboundaries3d/ (terms: https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/en/terms-of-use-free-geodata-and-geoservices)
  4. Federal Office of Topography swisstopo: swissNAMES3D, release 2026. Gives the names and positions of Basel, the Dufourspitze and the labelled places. Same terms. https://data.geo.admin.ch/ch.swisstopo.swissnames3d/
  5. Federal Office of Topography swisstopo: national map height of the Dufourspitze, 4,634 m, as quoted in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dufourspitze
